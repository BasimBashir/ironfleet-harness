Expected bench questions: breach of statutory duty cause of action

Q1. Does Part 16 of the Companies Act 1993 impose a duty on the liquidator that the plaintiff can enforce in
damages?
A. No. In Henderson v Walker [2019] NZHC 2184 the High Court held that the notion of breach of statutory duty
is that a positive obligation has not been complied with. The liquidation provisions confer positive powers
and duties; they do not say a liquidator may act only in the specified way.

Q2. If the liquidator went beyond the powers in the Act, is that not a breach of duty?
A. Not a breach of statutory duty. Exceeding a power is not failing to perform a positive obligation; any
remedy for acting outside power lies in other forms of civil liability, which are separately pleaded.

Q3. What does the structure of Part 16 tell the Court about Parliament's intention?
A. Part 16 provides alternative modes of enforcement, in particular Companies Act 1993, s 286, under which an
affected person may apply for an order requiring the liquidator to comply with his or her duties, backed by
removal and prohibition. Providing that statutory mode of enforcement is evidence Parliament did not intend a
private action for damages.

Q4. So how should the Court dispose of this cause of action?
A. As in Henderson v Walker, it fails: the plaintiff has identified no positive obligation the liquidator
failed to perform, and the s 286 route, not damages, is the remedy the Act provides.
