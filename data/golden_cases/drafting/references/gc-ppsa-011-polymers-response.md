Your client's claimed security interest

We act for the liquidators and respond to your letter. We do not accept that your client holds a perfected
security interest in the stock, and the liquidators decline to recognise the claimed priority.

The Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 149 provides that a registration is invalid if a defect in the
financing statement is seriously misleading; ss 150 and 151 add that errors in the debtor's details can be
seriously misleading without proof that anyone was actually misled. Whether an error is seriously misleading
is tested against the search criteria in the Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 172, which for a
company debtor include its incorporation number.

Your client's financing statement classified the debtor as an "other organisation" rather than a company and
left the incorporation number blank. A search of the register by the debtor's company number would not
retrieve that registration. That is exactly the error considered in Polymers International Ltd v Toon [2013]
NZHC 1897, where the High Court held that a failure to record the debtor as a company and to enter its
incorporation number was seriously misleading, so the supplier's financing statement was not validly
registered, and declined the relief sought, with costs.

The same result follows here: your client's financing statement is not validly registered, its security
interest is unperfected, and in the liquidation it ranks as an unsecured creditor. If your client disputes
this, please identify any authority you say distinguishes Polymers.
