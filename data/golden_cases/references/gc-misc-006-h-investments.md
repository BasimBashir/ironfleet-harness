Your proof of debt must be made out to the ordinary civil standard, and the onus stays with you.

Sections 233 to 242 of the Insolvency Act 2006 govern a creditor's claim form, and Insolvency Act 2006, s 236 empowers the
Official Assignee to require further evidence or summon persons for examination; but nothing in that regime
transfers the burden of proving a provable debt from the claimant creditor. The Assignee performs a
quasi-judicial function and there is no lesser "arguable case" standard of the kind applied when leave is sought
under s 284 of the Companies Act 1993 for liquidator directions.

The claimant bears an evidential onus throughout: allegations are not self-proving, and unrebutted allegations do
not shift the onus to the bankrupt or the Assignee simply because he has chosen not to respond.

The standard is balance of probabilities, as Ferreira v Stockinger [2015] NZHC 2916 confirms for formal proof:
the court must be satisfied by evidence that would have been necessary had the matter gone to trial.

On the merits, merely trading on while not accounting for GST is not of itself a breach of director's duties;
a proof alleging only that fails. Expect rejection unless evidence of loss causation is filed — as in H Investments
Limited (in liquidation) v Official Assignee [2018] NZCA 76 where the appeal was dismissed.
