The registration was not validly made, so the security interest is unperfected against third parties.

Under Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 149 a defect, irregularity or omission in a
financing statement does not affect the validity of registration unless it is seriously misleading;
Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 150 makes registration invalid where such an error occurs
in the debtor's name as required by s 142, and s 151 confirms no proof that anyone was actually misled is needed.

Whether an error is seriously misleading is assessed functionally: the question is whether the
information recorded still allows an effective search under Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 172,
which permits searching by debtor name only if the debtor is correctly described.

Recording the debtor as an unincorporated organisation meant no company number could be entered,
and a searcher using the company number — often the cheapest and most reliable check for a trading
company — would find nothing. That is precisely how Polymers International Ltd v Toon [2013] NZHC 1897
was decided: Asher J held two linked errors (wrong organisation type and missing company number)
seriously misleading, refused the declaration of validity sought, and left the supplier unable to
assert a perfected security interest against the liquidators.

Advise the client that in this case its filing should be treated as invalid.
