Do not sue yet, and do not ask the court to compel a proceeding: first obtain the fully informed views of shareholders.

Whether to litigate in the company's name is the liquidators' commercial decision, but where it will be funded
by shareholders' money and put their funds at risk on costs, those views matter greatly. In Hedley v Albany Power
Centre Ltd (in liq) [2006] 2 NZCCLR 1148 Wild J held that if a simple majority of shareholders, fully informed,
did not want a proceeding against the directors that would conclude the matter; he would not direct liquidators
to sue contrary to such a wish.

"Informed" means real information: the amount potentially recoverable, prospects of success, estimated costs and
likely time frame. Shareholding directors (and interests they own) are excluded when counting the majority,
which is assessed under Companies Act 1993, s 105(2).

A shareholder cannot by-pass this: her own route is leave under Companies Act 1993, s 165 to bring a derivative
proceeding, or she may seek a direction under Companies Act 1993, s 284. If instead a significant minority supports
suing, consider funding by those shareholders against an indemnity.
