No. Your client cannot recover the stallion; the bank's registered interest has priority over the
ownership retained.

Because the lease runs for more than one year, the retention of title is for priority purposes a
security interest (Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 17(1)(b)) even though it secures nothing.
The Act ranks interests by registration, not by title.

The lessee's statutory rights defeat you. Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 40 provides, in
s 40(3), that a debtor has rights in goods leased to the debtor, so the stud acquired rights in goods on
taking possession - rights that did not exist before the Act commenced and that are after-acquired property
within the bank's all-assets debenture. The security agreement binds your client as a third
party because the stud signed a charge over all its present and future property (s 36(1)(b)(ii)), value
had been given, so the interest attached under s 40(1) and was perfected by registration (s 41).

Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 66 then gives a perfected security interest priority over an
unperfected one in the same collateral. Your client never registered, so its interest is unperfected and
loses; nemo dat does not assist, since exceptions to it are the foundation of the regime. Only an account
of any surplus after the bank's debt is met survives.
