No. The new facility ranks behind the bank's 2019 charge and no super-priority is available on the
refinance.

A purchase money security interest is defined in Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 16 as an
interest taken by a seller to secure the collateral's purchase price, or by a lender whose value enables
the debtor to acquire rights in the collateral. Money advanced to pay out an existing facility does
neither in substance: the debtor gains no greater right to the economic value of the collateral, merely
exchanging one creditor for another, and the asset pool is not enlarged - which is the whole justification
for super priority. Nor is the original interest carried across, since separate security was taken for the
new advances, negativing any intention to retain it; the earlier purchase money security interest was
extinguished when the payout was made.

Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 73 is a conclusive further obstacle. It gives a purchase money
security interest in collateral other than inventory or intangibles priority over a non-purchase money
security interest of the same debtor only if it is perfected not later than 10 working days after the day
on which the debtor obtained possession of the collateral. The breeder has had possession of the stallion
for years, so a refinance-created interest could never meet that window.

The client holds only an ordinary security interest, perfected when it registered, and subordinate to the
bank's earlier perfected charge.
