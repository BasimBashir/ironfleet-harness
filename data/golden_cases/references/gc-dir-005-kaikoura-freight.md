The director's personal liability is measured by the losses his breaches caused, not by the company's whole shortfall.

Under Companies Act 1993, s 301 the High Court may order a director who has breached his duties to pay
compensation to the company for loss suffered by it. Where the losses are the company's debts to creditors,
the enquiry is how far the director's breaches caused or enlarged that indebtedness.

Here reckless trading under s 135 (carrying on the business in a manner creating a substantial risk of
serious loss to creditors) and breach of s 136 (incurring obligations without reasonable grounds to believe
they would be met) were established from 31 October 2012, and creditor claims admitted at $340,837.01.

Quantification follows Lower v Traveller [2005] 3 NZLR 479: there must be a causal link between the reckless
trading and the debts relied on, judged by asking how much the liabilities increased because trading continued
after the date on which the director knew cessation was required.

Culpability, deterrence and the duration of the wrongful trading then locate the director within the range —
continuing to trade for years while penalties and interest accrued to Inland Revenue is mid to upper range —
but the award cannot exceed the loss caused by the known reckless trading.

Losses from legitimate commercial risk fall outside the award, so the director is not made to underwrite a
"fair share" outcome; note too that s 284 directions valuing a claim at zero were refused.
