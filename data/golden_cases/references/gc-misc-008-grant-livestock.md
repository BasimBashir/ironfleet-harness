No. There is no jurisdiction to combine the assets into one fund, and the farmers' remedy is a personal
order against the director.

A pooling order under Companies Act 1993, s 271 can be made only in respect of related companies, and
"related" is defined by reference to common membership of a group of bodies corporate. Carrying on the
same business under a similar name, with the same director, from the same premises and off the same client
ledger, does not make the new company related to the failed one. With no common shareholders the
jurisdictional precondition simply fails, so there is no discretion to merge the two estates.

The phoenix provisions do not fill the gap: s 386A prohibits a director of a failed company from being
concerned in the management of a phoenix company of similar name, but Companies Act 1993, s 386C confines
the personal liability it creates to the relevant debts of the phoenix company itself. Without a pooling
order those are not the debts of the failed agency, so that cause of action fails too.

What remains is relief against the director personally. Withholding and dispersing client sale proceeds
held on trust, while asserting a fictitious commission, is a serious breach of the duty to act in good
faith in the company's interests and warrants an order that he pay the money with interest. It is not
reckless trading: the conduct was deliberate evasion of known creditors, not the taking of an
illegitimate commercial risk to trade out of jeopardy.
