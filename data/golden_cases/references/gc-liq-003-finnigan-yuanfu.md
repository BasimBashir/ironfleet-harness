No. The pooled money is not part of the company's estate for distribution to its other creditors.

Directions should be sought under Companies Act 1993, s 284. Because the client agreement provided that
the company would hold client money on trust and that clients retained control over how it was traded,
the clients kept the beneficial interest and the company was a trustee, not merely a debtor. Mixing the
client money with the company's own fee income does not defeat that: following Re Hallett's Estate the
company is presumed to have spent its own money first. That makes the whole fund trust property,
not available for other creditors.

Distribution among the clients follows equitable principles rather than the statutory pari passu scheme
in Part 16. Re Registered Securities Ltd (in liq) [1991] 1 NZLR 545 rejects the rule in Clayton's Case
as between beneficiaries of a mixed fund: it rests on presumed intention and must give way where it would
work an injustice, and here the poor records would make first-in first-out unworkable and arbitrary. The
North American rolling-charge method is also inappropriate for the same evidential reason.

The fund should therefore be distributed pari passu among the clients in proportion to the amounts each
contributed, with two qualifications: settled or agreed claims are not disturbed, and any client who can
actually trace his money to an identified asset may still do so. The liquidators are entitled to be paid
their remuneration out of the trust assets in the court's inherent jurisdiction before distribution,
apportioned between trust and non-trust work.
