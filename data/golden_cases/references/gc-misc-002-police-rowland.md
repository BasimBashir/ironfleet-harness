Yes: both settlements should be approved, and in the terms the parties have agreed.

The power is Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, s 95, which allows approval of a settlement on two
cumulative grounds, that it is consistent with the purposes of the Act and that it accords with the overall
interests of justice, and each settlement must be justified on its own terms.

The first settlement meets both. The man concedes he benefited from significant criminal activity, and
Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, s 53 measures the unlawful benefit, agreed at $1,320,000, which is
also the maximum recoverable amount. Because the whole of his property goes, nothing a larger order could
have reached is surrendered, so the compromise gives the Crown all it could have obtained while saving the
cost of litigating the extent of his interest.

The partner's settlement is reached the same way. Criminal Proceeds (Recovery) Act 2009, s 58 treats property
as the respondent's even though it is registered in or held by another, if it is under his effective control;
her admission that he ran everything means her disclaimer costs her nothing, and all of it is realised under
the profit forfeiture order.

The car alone is dealt with differently, and the compromise is orthodox. Only the $13,000 traceable to her
own dealing is tainted, so that sum is forfeited by asset forfeiture order and paid into the
proceeds-of-crime account, while the balance, from legitimate borrowing, stays with the vehicle; her father,
the innocent registered owner, consents to its return, so no stranger to the offending is penalised.

Both settlements are therefore consistent with the purposes of the Act and the overall interests of justice.
