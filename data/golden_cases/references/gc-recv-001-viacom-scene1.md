Refuse the stay: the receivers should sell now and hold the proceeds for whoever proves entitled to them.

The factors come from Dymocks Franchise Systems (NSW) Pty Ltd v Bilgola Enterprises Ltd (1999) 13 PRNZ 48:
whether the appeal would be rendered nugatory, the appellant's bona fides, injury to the successful party,
third-party effects, novelty, the public interest, and the overall balance of convenience.

The first factor fails the appellant: its complaints, that discount selling will depress the value of its
rights and injure retailers stocking its product, sound in damages and are compensable if it wins. The
receivers' undertaking to hold the proceeds undisbursed until judgment preserves the fund the appeal is
about, so the right of appeal remains effective and the appeal is not nugatory.

The counterweights carry the day. Receivers must realise charged stock in a timely fashion for the secured
creditor and creditors generally: $7.9m is owed to the bank, $12.4m to unsecured creditors, and warehousing
of $9,400 a month accrues while the stock loses value, so delay would impoverish the estate.

That is also the balance of convenience. Consistently with New Zealand Insulators Ltd v ABB, which applied
Minnesota Mining & Manufacturing Co v Johnson & Johnson Ltd [1976] RPC 671, a court faced with an arguable
appeal does not determine it prematurely; undertakings hold a sensible and just balance pending decision.

The application is dismissed, with the receivers' undertaking as to proceeds recorded in the order.
