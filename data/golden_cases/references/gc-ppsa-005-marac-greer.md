The bank takes the rents, and the PPSA does not govern the dispute between the lenders.

Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 23(e)(ii) excludes from the Act a security interest created by a
transfer of a right to payment that arises in connection with an interest in land, including a transfer of
rental payments payable under a lease of or licence to occupy land. An absolute assignment of the rent by
way of security in a memorandum of mortgage is exactly that: it is absolute even though taken as part of
the mortgage security, the exclusion is not narrowly construed, and the licence letting the owner keep
collecting the rent does not disturb it, since that licence ends on default.

The bank's conduct does not change the position. Appointing a receiver of income rather than entering
possession, and acting before any Property Law Act 2007 s 119 notice expired, sufficed: possession is not
a precondition of the mortgagee's entitlement to the rent.

Because s 23 excludes the mortgage from the Act, priority falls to be determined outside the PPSA, so
Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 66 (perfected first, then order of registration) is never engaged
and the 2023 registration date is irrelevant. The first-ranking land mortgagee with the assignment of rent
takes the rents from the date it enforced; your client proves in the debtor's estate for any shortfall.
