Release the funds on terms, and do not treat the liquidation as at an end.

Where a judgment debtor has paid into court unconditionally while an appeal is pending,
the money should not simply sit frozen. The court will order it disbursed to the liquidators provided they
give undertakings binding them to repay if the appeal succeeds. An application of this kind is brought under
Part 20, Subpart 3 of the High Court Rules 2008.

The course taken in Ranolf Company Ltd (in liq) v Bhana [2017] NZHC 1183 is instructive: once $298,500 was
paid into court against a charging order of $312,450 obtained by Gilbert J, the liquidators undertook to repay
the judgment sum, interest or lesser amounts as the Court of Appeal might order, and to instruct removal of
their caveats once the funds cleared into their trust account, and the funds were disbursed on those recorded undertakings.

So take the undertakings here, release the funds and lift caveats lodged over the trustees' land, and keep
approved enforcement costs out of the repayment promise.

As to termination of the liquidation, a bare request is not enough: it needs a formal application under the
Companies Act 1993 supported by affidavit evidence, which should be resisted pending the appeal.
