The bank is entitled to the buildings; your client's claim fails, and priority goes to the registered
charge.

A lease of goods for a term of more than one year is itself a security interest even though it secures
nothing. Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 17(1)(b) applies whether or not the lease secures
payment or performance, so the arrangement is a deemed security interest; and Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 16
defines "lease for a term of more than 1 year" to include a lease for an indefinite term and a lease of a
year or less where the lessee keeps uninterrupted possession beyond a year. This agreement is squarely
within that definition, so the ownership retained is treated, for priority purposes, as a security interest.

The bank's debenture is also a security interest, and because a financing statement was registered it is
a perfected security interest. Personal Property Securities Act 1999, s 66 gives a perfected security
interest priority over an unperfected one in the same collateral.

Your client's interest is unregistered, therefore unperfected, and therefore subordinate, even though it
remains the legal owner; s 40(3) also gave the lessee rights in goods, so the all-assets charge attached
to the buildings themselves. Registration was the only protection, and none was taken.
