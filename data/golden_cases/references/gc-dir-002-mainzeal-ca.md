Compensation for the breach of Companies Act 1993, s 136 is assessed on the new debt approach: the
debts incurred in breach are treated as a form of harm to the company, disregarding any offsetting
benefits the company obtained from the transactions that produced them.

The s 135 breach was confirmed but the claim to compensation for it fails. There was no net
deterioration in the company's position between the breach date and liquidation, the breach did not
cause the insolvency, and the "entire deficiency" approach was neither pleaded nor supported by the
evidence, so it would be unfair to impose liability on that basis.

The new debt approach is available for breach of s 136 because that section, unlike s 135, addresses
obligations incurred by individual creditors rather than the creditors as a class. It follows from
Madsen-Ries v Cooper [2020] NZSC 100 that, in order to make s 136 work in practice, the new debts
incurred in breach of that provision must be treated as a form of harm to the company.

The amount recoverable is not the gross new debt. Only the net deficit to the relevant creditors can
be recovered: allowance must be made for all payments those creditors received before or during the
liquidation other than as a result of these proceedings, and for dividends paid or payable from other
recoveries, with interest since liquidation still to be addressed. The figure is fixed by the High
Court on referral, which must also consider reduction in the exercise of the s 301 discretion and how
liability should be apportioned between the directors.
